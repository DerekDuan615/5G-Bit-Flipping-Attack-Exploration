We can see from the final XOR value, there are only three patterns:
(1) All 0s, which means attacked is not detected. 
    Since we flip the same index of bits on checksum and payload, and there is no keystream-based shuffle here, around 50% of message can bypass the detection.
(2) One contiguous block of 1s (multiple different length), but the lsb of 1 is always at the 9th least bit (index 8).
    This makes sense, because when we flip the 8th least bit (index 7) in the payload, we should have a contiguous block of 1s which starts at the 8th least bit (index 7)
    However, since we also flip the 8th least bit (index 7) of the checksum, it make the final contiguous block of 1s starts at the 9th least bit (index 8)
(3) Two contiguous blocks of 1s. Only 2 XOR values has this pattern out of 1000, which are '1111111100000001' and '1111111000111111'
    The lsb of 1 of the first block is always at the 9th least bit (index 8)
    We have the same conclusion that both the the 8th least bit (index 7) of payload and checksum is attacked, just overlook the second 1s block for now.
    If we see another case which has the same effect then we can discuss then.
